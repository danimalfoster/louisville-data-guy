Dimension tables hold the descriptive context for a [fact table](/glossary/fact-table/). If facts answer
"how much," dimensions answer "by what": by customer, by product, by region, by month. Almost every
filter, row label, and slicer on a report comes from a dimension.

Dimensions are usually much smaller than fact tables but much wider. A customer dimension might have
50 columns: name, segment, industry, city, state, account manager, signup date, and so on. The richer the
dimensions, the more questions people can answer without asking IT for a new report.

## Keys

Each dimension row gets a **surrogate key**, a simple number generated by the warehouse, in addition to
the **natural key** from the source system (like a customer number). Surrogate keys let the warehouse
keep several versions of the same customer over time (see
[slowly changing dimension](/glossary/slowly-changing-dimension/)) and combine customers from systems
whose IDs overlap.

## The date dimension

Almost every warehouse has a date dimension with one row per calendar day and columns like day of week,
fiscal quarter, holiday flag, and "is last business day of month." It looks unnecessary until finance
asks for results by fiscal week, and then it saves hours.

## An example

A `dim_product` table has one row per product, with SKU, product name, brand, category, subcategory,
size, color, and launch date. Any sales measure can now be broken down by any of those attributes.

## Where people get it wrong

**"Each team can build its own customer table."** When marketing, sales, and finance each define
customers differently, their reports never agree. Shared, *conformed* dimensions, ideally fed by
[master data management](/glossary/master-data-management/), are what make numbers match across
departments.
